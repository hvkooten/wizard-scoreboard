WIZARD

Players: 3 to 6 apprentices
Age: 10 and up
Time: Approximately 45 minutes
Designer Wizard: Ken Fisher
Designer App: Heimen van Kooten
Needed: Official Wizard card game (containing 60 character cards), Tablet of Truth (phone/tablet with this app), 1 parchment of rules

Once upon a time…

A long, long time ago, when there was still the famous Magician Academy in Stonehenge, the Apprentices had to learn this game, along with other exercises, to train their magic abilities. It served to develop and strengthen their gift of prediction. Over the years, a deeper understanding of the game was lost. An entertaining card game, often played in inns by workmen, farmers and soldiers, is all that remains. Only after the famous English archaeologist Dr. Hensch Stone dug deep beneath the standing stones of Stonehenge and discovered historical parchment scrolls in old vaults did the true history of Wizard come to light again. The following rules correspond with the wording of the age-old parchments. The illustrations on the cards give a feeling for the old participants.

The Task

With this magic pack of cards the Apprentices must predict the exact number of tricks they will win in each round. Experience Points are awarded for a correct forecast. Whoever has collected the most points at the end of the game wins and thereby ascends to the level of a wise Wizard.

The Preparation

One player is appointed the Confidant of the Apprentices. The Confidant receives the Tablet of Truth and conscientiously keeps track of the Experience Points awarded during the game. Before a single card is touched, the Confidant first prepares the Tablet.

Preparing the Tablet of Truth

  • Under Groups the Confidant founds a circle of Apprentices: a group name, the number of players (3 to 6) and their names.
  • The order is of great importance. Use the arrows to put the names in seating order, clockwise around the table. From this the Tablet knows who deals each round and in which order the predictions are asked. Therefore place the Apprentice who deals the first round at the top of the group; the others follow in the order in which they sit to his left.
  • For each group the Confidant also chooses how Trump is determined (Dealer picks trump, see Trump) and from which round the bid-not-total rule applies (see The Predictions).
  • With Start Game on the Scoreboard a group is chosen, followed by the Apprentices taking part in this game. Check once more that the first dealer is at the top.
  • Under Settings the Tablet is attuned to its master: language, theme (system, light or dark), trump suit style (card suits or the four colors), the default bid-not-total rule for new groups, keeping the screen on during a game, bold text for weary eyes, and simplified buttons with symbols instead of words.

Only when the Tablet is ready are the character cards shuffled and dealt by the dealer the Tablet indicates.

The Character Cards

  • There are four different colors: Humans (blue), Elves (green), Dwarves (red), Giants (yellow).
  • The strongest card in each color is the “13”, the weakest card is the “1”.
  • The four Wizard cards (“Z” or “W”) are always Trump. They are higher than every “13”.
  • The four Fool cards (“N”) are never Trump. They are lower than every “1”.

Distributing the Cards

  • In Wizard the Apprentices receive a different number of cards in each round.
  • In the first round only one card is dealt to each player. Therefore only one trick can be won in this round.
  • In the second round two cards are dealt to each player. In this round there are two tricks to win.
  • In the third round three cards are dealt to each player, then four cards in the fourth round, etc., until in the last round all cards are dealt out to the players.
  • Cards that are not dealt form a face-down deck in the middle of the table. Each round the role of dealer passes clockwise to the next Apprentice on the left.

Trump

There are two ways to determine Trump. Choose your way before the game begins.

Trump option 1

  • The dealer determines Trump. He may examine his cards first.

Trump option 2

  • After the cards are dealt, the top card of the deck is turned face up. This card determines the Trump color for the current round.
  • If the card is a Fool, there is no Trump in the current round.
  • If the card is a Wizard, the dealer determines Trump. He may examine his cards first.
  • The last round has no Trump, since there are no cards left.

On the Tablet of Truth

  • If Dealer picks trump is on, the group plays with Trump option 1. The dealer may then also declare that there is no Trump.
  • If the switch is off, the group plays with Trump option 2. In the last round the Tablet itself proclaims that there is no Trump.
  • At the start of each round the Confidant taps the Trump suit, shown as card suits or as the four colors, as chosen under Settings.

The Predictions

After looking at his cards, each Apprentice must predict how many tricks he will take in this round. In turn, each Apprentice tells his prediction to the Confidant, starting with the player to the left of the dealer. These bids are noted on the Tablet of Truth.

With Next Round the Confidant opens the round. The Tablet asks the Apprentices in the correct order, the dealer last, and shows the total of all predictions.

The bid-not-total rule

Some circles of Apprentices forbid the dealer to make the sum of all predictions equal to the number of tricks in the round, so that at least one Apprentice must miss. For each group the Confidant chooses from which round this rule applies: Disabled, after the number of players, after twice the number of players, or from a fixed round. The Tablet then warns which prediction the dealer may not make.

The Battle for the Trick

The player to the left of the dealer plays the first card of the first trick. The other Apprentices follow clockwise. The led color must be followed. If that is not possible, an Apprentice may throw off any color or play Trump.

Note: Wizard and Fool cards may always be played, even if a player could follow the led color. A player does not have to play them when he cannot follow the led color.

The highest card wins the trick (Wizard cards are higher than all other cards, even Trump cards). The winner opens the next trick. Exception: in the first round only one trick is played.

Who wins the trick:

  • The first Wizard played in the trick
  • Or the highest card in the Trump color
  • Or the highest card in the led color
  • Or the first Fool played in the trick

Special Rights of the Wizards and Fools

  • When a trick is opened with a Wizard, the following Apprentices may throw off any cards, including further Wizards and Fools.
  • The trick goes to the first Wizard played.
  • Wizards are Trump; however, they need not be played when the first card of a trick is an actual Trump color card.
  • If a trick is opened with a Fool, any card may be played as the second card. The second card’s color then determines the color that must be followed.
  • Fools lose every trick, with one exception: in a trick in which only Fools are played, the first Fool wins the trick. This is only possible with three or four players.

The Assignment of the Experience Points

The Apprentice who predicted the number of tricks won exactly receives 20 Experience Points plus 10 points per trick won. Anyone who missed their bid loses 10 Experience Points for each trick over or under their prediction.

When the last trick of the round has been played, the Confidant enters on the Tablet of Truth how many tricks each Apprentice has won. Together these must equal the number of tricks in the round. The Tablet then calculates the Experience Points itself. Did the Confidant make a mistake? With Edit last round the tricks won in the last round can be corrected; the predictions and Trump remain as they were.

Example

First round
Thomas predicted that he would not take a trick. He was right and receives 20 points. Ute wanted the trick, but did not get it. She loses 10 points. Kevin predicted that he would take the trick; he was right and receives 30 points with the trick.

Second round
Thomas predicted both tricks for himself, but got only one. Result: he loses 10 points. Ute wanted no tricks and was right. She receives 20 points. Kevin also predicted no tricks for himself. However, he got one and therefore loses 10 points. The points are noted and added to the points from the previous rounds.

The End

There are 60 character cards in the game. The Apprentices play until, in the last round, all cards are dealt out. With 6 players that is 10 rounds, with 5 players 12, with 4 players 15, and with 3 players 20 rounds. The last round is also scored. The Apprentice with the highest Experience score wins.

Resting and ending

  • Must the Apprentices interrupt their training? With Pause the game is safely stored. Under Saved Games it can be opened later and continued with Resume.
  • With End Game a game can be ended before the last round. The Apprentice with the most points at that moment wins.
  • Under Highscore the Tablet keeps, for each Apprentice, the number of victories, the number of games played and the best score ever.
